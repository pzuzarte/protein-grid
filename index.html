    Patterns

      Protein Grid help

      A pattern grid and sound editor for the Waldorf Protein. It runs in your browser and sends MIDI to the Protein. Your patterns, sounds and mappings stay on this computer. It needs a current Chrome or Edge on a computer. It's unofficial and not affiliated with Waldorf.

      Visits to this page are counted anonymously with GoatCounter, which uses no cookies. It never receives your patterns or sounds.

      Recording knob turns

      • With Rec on and the pattern playing, turning any setting mapped on the Synth tab on the Protein records its movement into this pattern's Knobs lane, where in the pattern you turned it. Each time round the loop, the new movements replace what was there at those points. They then play back like drawn ones.
      • While you're turning a knob, its lane isn't played back, so the two don't fight. Undo takes back the whole take, notes and knob movements together. With the Knobs lane open, it shows the knob you're turning.

      A sound for each pattern

      • Sound, beside the patterns, chooses the sound a pattern plays with. Whenever you load that pattern or switch to it, by clicking its number, in a chain or from Song Grid, its sound is sent to the Protein: its settings that are mapped on the Synth tab are sent, so the rest of the sound stays as it is. Sounds from the sound library can be chosen once Teach reading has been done.
      • Play doesn't send it again, so a patch you choose on the synth while it plays stays until you switch patterns. To keep a different sound with the pattern, choose it in Sound and click Save.
      • Whatever's loaded sends nothing, so the pattern plays with the sound the Protein has at the time.
      • The pattern keeps its own copy of the sound, so it isn't changed by renaming or deleting the sound later. Save saves it with the pattern, and Duplicate copies it.

      Set up

      1. Connect the Protein to your computer with its USB-C cable, and turn it on.
      2. Allow MIDI access when asked. The MIDI badge turns green when the Protein is found. If it says MIDI blocked, click the icon to the left of the address, set MIDI devices to Allow, then click Allow MIDI.
      3. Click a note name in the grid. You should hear it on the Protein.
      4. Check that Channel, at the top right, matches the Protein's MIDI channel in its Settings.

      Mapping the Protein's settings

      • The Protein has no fixed MIDI controller numbers for its settings. Each one is mapped on the synth itself, so the Synth tab's sliders do nothing until their setting is mapped.
      • Set up controls walks you through them one at a time, the most useful first. For each one: turn that setting on the Protein, press Shift + Settings, choose Map CC and press the Select encoder, then click Send CC here. The page picks a free controller number for it.
      • Sending also sets that setting to the slider's value, or the middle if it isn't known yet.
      • Skip moves on without mapping, and Done stops. You can carry on later, and the mapped count shows how far you are. Map next to any slider maps just that one, or maps it again.
      • The Protein lists its mappings under Shift + Settings, Mappings, where you can delete one. Forget mapping only forgets it here.
      • The Protein gives no sign over MIDI that a mapping worked, so move the slider afterwards and check that the setting changes. If it doesn't, map it again.
      • Each of the Protein's four layers needs its own mappings, as a mapping made on layer A only moves layer A. Choose a layer with Layer on the Synth tab, select the same layer on the Protein, then map as above. Every mapping takes its own controller number, and there are 89 to share between the four layers, so map the settings you'll use on each layer rather than all 52 on all four. The two effect amounts are shared by every layer on the Protein, so they're mapped once and show on every layer. Hover over the mapped count to see how many are left.
      • Mappings are kept in this browser and in backups. Whether the Protein keeps them through a restart, or for every sound, hasn't been checked yet.

      Synth tab

      • Layer A to D picks which of the Protein's layers the sliders show and control. Each layer keeps its own values and mappings. A faint letter has nothing mapped yet. The Effects sliders are the same on every layer, as the Protein shares its effects between them. How the layers play together (one at a time, Layered, Round Robin, Random Robin or Midi Split) is set on the Protein with its Multi button.
      • Moving a slider sends its setting straight away, even during playback. Values run from 0 to 127, and the Protein spreads that over the setting's own range, so its display shows the exact value. Settings with a middle point show 0 there.
      • A slider shown faint hasn't been set from this page yet. The Protein can't report its settings, so the page doesn't know them until you move them or load a sound.
      • Save sound keeps the sliders' values in this browser under a name, and Load sends them back. Only the mapped settings are sent. A saved sound can be loaded onto any layer. These don't replace the Protein's own presets.
      • Vary nudges the known settings a little or a lot, leaving tuning, pan and volume alone. Hear original flips between the sound before you started and the new one. Both work on the layer shown.
      • Read from Protein and Teach reading cover layer A for now. Read from Protein updates the sliders from the Protein's current sound, such as after choosing a preset there. Click it, then on the Protein press Presets, open the Actions menu and choose Send. Chrome asks for SysEx permission the first time. The Protein can't be asked for its sound, so it takes that press of Send each time.
      • Teach reading is needed once, after mapping, and again if you change mappings. It takes two presses of Send: the first keeps your sound, then the page sets each mapped setting to a test value, and the second shows where each one is in the Protein's dump. Your sound is put back afterwards. Settings it can't find are named in the status line. What it learns is kept in this browser.
      • Save dump keeps the last sound the Protein sent as a .syx file, and Add to library saves it in the Library tab's folder.
      • Mod slots' sources and targets, effect types and their deeper settings, and the arpeggiator and sequencer are set on the Protein.

      Library tab

      • The bar at the top of the Grid tab shows the name of the pattern you're on, large. Save (Cmd+S) saves it to its file again, under the same name, with its notes, bars, knob movements, tempo and sound. The first time, and with Save as… (Cmd+Shift+S), the Mac's Save dialog asks for a name and a folder. It starts in the library folder's Patterns folder, then in wherever you saved last. Open… opens a pattern file from anywhere into the pattern you're on. Saved patterns goes to the patterns in the library folder on the Library tab, where Load puts one into the pattern you're on. A pattern you load from there or open saves back to its own file. Duplicate makes a copy that doesn't, so its first Save asks where. Undo brings back what was there before a load.
      • The library keeps whole sounds, every setting and layer, exactly as the Protein sent them. It's a folder on this computer that you choose with Choose library folder, and each sound is saved there as its own .syx file, named after the sound. That keeps them even if the browser's data is cleared, and your usual backups include them. Where each came from is kept in library-info.json in the same folder. After Chrome restarts, click Allow library folder to use it again.
      • This is separate from Save sound on the Synth tab, which only keeps the mapped settings, in this browser.
      • Add from Protein waits for a sound. On the Protein, press Presets, open the Actions menu and choose Send. The file is named after the sound, and Rename renames the file, not the sound on the Protein. Click the star beside a sound to make it a favourite, and Favourites beside the search shows only those. Favourites are kept in library-info.json in the library folder, so they stay with your sounds. .syx files you put in the folder yourself show up too.
      • Capture presets fills the library with the Protein's presets, its factory ones included. The Protein can't be asked for its sounds, so it needs a Send for each: click Capture presets, then on the Protein choose a preset, press Presets, open the Actions menu and choose Send, and go on to the next. Each is saved as it arrives, named after the sound. Sounds already in the library are skipped, and the count shows how many were saved. If one doesn't arrive whole, it isn't saved, and the status says so: send it again. Click Stop capture when you're done. The Protein sends presets from slot 117 up with nothing in them, even when they hold a sound, so to capture one of those, save it into a free slot at 116 or below on the Protein and send it from there.
      • Load settings sends the sound's mapped settings as controllers, and shows them on the Synth tab. It needs Teach reading to have been done. Settings that aren't mapped stay as they are on the Protein.
      • Send whole sound sends the complete dump back to the Protein. This hasn't been tried on a Protein yet. It may replace the sound you're playing, and it isn't known whether it also writes over a memory slot, so keep a copy of anything you want first.
      • Import .syx adds every sound in a file to the folder, and Export all downloads the whole library as one file.

      Working in a key

      • Choose a Key and Scale. The grid only shows notes in that key, so anything you draw fits. Move notes with key moves your patterns into a new key when you change it. All notes shows every note. Off, first under Key or Scale, turns the key off: the grid shows every note, and nothing is marked as in or out of a key. Notes stay where they are when you turn it off. Choosing a key again, with Move notes with key on, moves each note to the nearest one in that key and scale, without changing the root. With it off, chords are built from semitones, and Generate needs a key. Imported MIDI files come in with the key off, so every note shows as written, and the status line suggests the key they sound in. Choose a key and scale afterwards to fit them to it.
      • Draw places a single note, a power chord, a triad or a 7th, using notes from the key.
      • Idea and Generate write a bassline, melody, arpeggio or chord pattern in the key, and Undo brings back the old one.
      • Shift moves the whole pattern a step earlier or later, Scale moves every note a step through the scale, and Oct moves them an octave.
      • Click or drag along the step numbers to choose steps, then Copy and Paste. With no steps chosen, Copy takes the whole pattern and Paste puts it at the start. They work between S-1, TD-3, Bass Station and Protein Grid too, open in the same browser: copy in one and paste in another. A TD-3's accents and slides don't come across. Velocity and Set change the chosen steps, or the whole pattern.

      Mouse and keyboard

      Click a stepAdd or remove it. New steps use the Length setting, or one step when it's Off.
      Drag from an empty stepDraw one note as long as you drag. Hold Alt for single ticks.
      Drag a noteDrag the left of its first step, or the marker at its start, to move where it starts, keeping its end where it is. Drag the rest of its first step, or the marker at its end, to change where it ends. Both move in eighths of a step, or hold Alt for single ticks.
      Cmd-drag across stepsFill separate steps, or remove them when starting from a filled step.
      Right-click a stepEdit velocity, length, timing, probability and sub-steps.
      Shift-click a stepCycle velocity: full, 70%, 40%.
      SpacePlay and stop, unless you're typing in a text box.
      TTurn triplets on or off.
      Cmd or Ctrl + ZUndo.
      ? and EscOpen this help, and close it or the step editor.

      Chains, knob movements and files

      • Sub-step, in the step editor, plays a step in quick repeats, where o plays and - is silent.
      • Rec records notes into the pattern from the input and channel chosen beside it: the Protein's own keys, another keyboard, or any input on any channel. While playing, notes land exactly where you play them, or on the nearest step with Snap to steps chosen beside Rec, and end where you let go: they last as long as you hold them, whatever Length is set to, and each time round the loop adds to what's there. A note you're still holding isn't played back over itself. They then play back on the Protein like any other steps. While stopped, each key adds a note, as long as Length sets, or one step with Length Off, and moves on a step, starting from the chosen step. Undo takes back the whole take.
      • Click plays a metronome click on each beat while the pattern plays, louder on the first beat of each bar. It comes from this computer's speakers, not the instrument, and stays on or off for next time.
      • While recording, notes from another keyboard are passed on to the Protein so you hear them. If MIDI Hub is also routing that keyboard to the Protein, you'll hear each note twice, so choose one.
      • Chain takes pattern numbers such as 1 1 2 3. Tick On and Play goes through them in order, looping.
      • Knobs opens a lane under the grid to draw how a mapped setting moves through the pattern, on any layer: the list names each setting's layer first. Alt-drag erases, and Copy all to copies the movements to another pattern.
      • Patterns 1-16 are stored in this browser. Back up saves them with your saved sounds and mappings to one file, and Restore loads it back. Export MIDI and Import MIDI work with one pattern at a time.
      • Sync plays this page together with the other grids, S-1 Grid, Protein Grid, Bass Station Grid, MPC Grid and TD-3 Grid, when they're open in the same browser, each with its MIDI connected and Sync ticked. Pressing Play or Stop on any of them starts or stops them all, from the start of their patterns, at the tempo of the page you pressed, and changing the BPM on one changes them all. The badge beside Sync shows which pages are linked. Sync starts off each time the page opens, so tick it to link the page. Song Grid arranges their patterns into a song and plays them through Sync. The Tools menu at the top opens any of them.
      • Send clock sends MIDI clock with playback. With Sync Source set to Extern in the Protein's settings, its arpeggiator, sequencer and clocked LFOs follow this page's tempo.

      If you hear nothing

      • Check that the MIDI badge is green, and reload the page if the Protein was off when it opened.
      • Check the channel, and the Protein's volume.
      • Use Panic if a note gets stuck.
